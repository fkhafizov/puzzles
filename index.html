How far is far?

2×2×2 · full BFS · 2026-08-21
Hover a bar for the exact count.

In the half-turn metric the distribution peaks at $d = 9$ and then falls off a cliff: just 2,644 of 3,674,160 states — about 0.07% — are genuinely 11 moves from solved. The antipodal set is tiny, which is exactly why IDA* with a decent pruning table works so well here. The quarter-turn metric stretches the same group out two moves further and ends on 276 states at $d = 14$.

Two diameters, one group

$\operatorname{diam}_{\mathrm{HTM}} G_2 = 11$ and $\operatorname{diam}_{\mathrm{QTM}} G_2 = 14$. Charging 2 for a half turn instead of 1 adds three moves to the worst case.

House rules

Composition reads left to right

$\texttt{U R}$ means apply $U$ first, then $R$ — a right action, $\sigma \mapsto \sigma\cdot U\cdot R$. Every Math panel says so, because this is the usual reason a picture and a permutation printout disagree.

Say which metric

HTM counts $X$, $X'$ and $X2$ as one move each. QTM charges 2 for $X2$. Never compare a diameter in one metric against a bound in the other.

Faces are directions, not colors

$U$ white, $D$ yellow, $F$ green, $B$ blue, $R$ red, $L$ orange — the standard Western scheme, with $U$, $F$, $R$ meeting clockwise at a corner.

Factored form first

Group orders appear as products before decimals, and anything past $10^{20}$ gets a four-figure scientific approximation instead of a wall of digits.
