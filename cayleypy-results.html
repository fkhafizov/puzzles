CayleyPy — results, papers, and open boards

The CayleyPy collaboration trains neural networks to estimate diffusion distance on Cayley graphs and steers a GPU beam search with them. On puzzles far past the 3×3×3 cube it finds the shortest paths anyone has found. What it does not yet do — for the 4×4×4, for the Megaminx — is prove that no shorter path exists.

The graphs these searches run on

A logarithmic scale, because nothing else fits. Each marker is a puzzle whose Cayley graph CayleyPy searches; the count is the number of reachable states. Hover or tap a marker.

The 55-pancake graph carries about 3×1073 states — on this scale it sits a short step below the number of atoms in the observable universe, and a beam search still crosses it in minutes.

With more compute, on the 3×3×3

  • Optimality rate above 98% in the quarter-turn metric computed — against 60.3% for DeepCubeA and 69.6% for EfficientCube on the same benchmark.
  • Path finding roughly 20× faster than the previous state of the art at comparable solution lengths, and more than 26× faster end-to-end, using up to 18.5× less training time.
  • Shorter average solutions than the combined best Kaggle Santa 2023 results on the 3×3×3, 4×4×4 and 5×5×5 — every single case, across a field of more than a thousand teams.

Proved, conjectured, open

The distinction the Kaggle boards make visible. A diameter is a theorem about every state at once; a leaderboard entry is a witness for one scramble.

  • 3×3×3, half turns literature

    Diameter 20. Settled in 2010 by Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson and Dethridge and published in SIAM Review in 2014. Every position solvable in 20 moves; some need all 20.

  • 3×3×3, quarter turns literature

    Diameter 26. Rokicki and Davidson, 2014, in the metric where a half turn counts as two moves.

  • Symmetric group Sn literature

    Quasi-polynomial diameters, conjectured. For many generating sets the diameter appears to follow a small family of quadratic or linear polynomials indexed by n mod s. CayleyPy proposes a sharpening of the Babai-type bound to n2/2 + 4n in the undirected case, where earlier work gave only O(n2).

  • 4×4×4 cube open

    No diameter. Forty years on, only ever-shorter solutions. The best known upper bound is whatever the current leaderboard holds.

  • Megaminx open

    No diameter. Unsettled since 1982.

  • Professor Tetraminx open

    Order not yet pinned down in this project, let alone the diameter.

The gap is not a gap in effort. Proving a diameter of d means certifying that no state anywhere in the graph needs more than d moves — a statement over 1045 states for the 4×4×4. Beam search produces witnesses, one at a time, and a witness never closes a universal claim.

The papers

Four, in sequence, each building on the last — from a solver, to a library, to a conjectured duality that reframes what the search is doing.

I

A Machine Learning Approach That Beats Large Rubik's Cubes

arXiv:2502.13266 · 12 pages · cs.LG, cs.DM · NeurIPS 2025, spotlight

12 authorsAlexander Chervov, Kirill Khoruzhii, Nikita Bukhal, Jalal Naghiyev, Vladislav Zamkovoy, Ivan Koltsov, Lyudmila Cheldieva, Arsenii Sychev, Arsenii Lenin, Mark Obozov, Egor Urvanov, Alexey Romanov

  • A neural network estimates diffusion distance; a batched beam search uses those estimates as its heuristic. No handcrafted rules, no domain knowledge.
  • The first machine learning solver to work past the 3×3×3, with solution lengths on the 4×4×4 and 5×5×5 shorter than any previously available solver produced.
  • Above 98% optimality on the 3×3×3, where DeepCubeA reached 60.3% and EfficientCube 69.6%.

CayleyPy-4: AI-Holography. Towards analogs of holographic string dualities for AI tasks

arXiv:2603.22195 · March 2026 · 20+120 pages · hep-th, cs.AI, cs.LG, math.CO, math.GR

44 authorsA. Chervov, F. Levkovich-Maslyuk, A. Smolensky, F. Khafizov, I. Kiselev, D. Melnikov, I. Koltsov, S. Kudashev, D. Shiltsov, M. Obozov, S. Krymskii, V. Kirova, E. V. Konstantinova, A. Soibelman, S. Galkin, L. Grunwald, A. Kotov, A. Alexandrov, S. Lytkin, D. Fedoriaka, A. Chevychelov, Z. Kogan, A. Natyrova, L. Cheldieva, O. Nikitina, S. Fironov, A. Vakhrushev, A. Lukyanenko, V. Ilin, D. Gorodkov, N. Bogachev, I. Gaiur, M. Zaitsev, F. Petrov, L. Petrov, T. Gaintseva, A. Gavrilova, M. N. Smirnov, N. Kalinin, A. Khan, K. Jung, H. Mousset, H. Isambert, O. Debeaupuis

  • Proposes a discrete analogue of holographic string duality for Cayley graphs, on the reading that predicting the next move on a graph — what a GPT-style model or an RL agent does — is the analogue of predicting a particle trajectory.
  • For Cayley graphs of Sn the dual objects appear to be flat planar polygons, and the diameter equals the number of integer points inside the polygon, scaled by n.
  • A vertex maps holographically to a path inside that polygon, and graph distance becomes the area under the path — a discrete realisation of the complexity-equals-volume picture.
  • Evidence for continuous conformal field theories and dual strings in the large-n limit, with new datasets released to help establish the duality for more general cases.
  • The duality feeds back into the earlier conjectures: it leads to variants of the Babai bound and to the quasi-polynomiality of diameters and word metrics from paper III.
